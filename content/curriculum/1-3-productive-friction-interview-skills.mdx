## Why Ad-Hoc Prompting Manufactures Hallucinations

Watch how most employees use AI at work: they type *"Draft a Q4 vendor renewal negotiation memo for Acme"* or *"Scope a client onboarding plan"* in twelve words.

Because language models are trained to be helpful immediately, **an under-specified prompt forces the model to invent the missing 85% of reality**: your pricing floors, legal walk-away clauses, historical margin targets, and stakeholder politics. People call the result a "hallucination," when in reality it is **unconstrained guesswork caused by zero intake friction**.

## The Principle of Productive Friction (Interview-First Architecture)

High-reliability AI systems flip the interaction pattern. Instead of generating on Turn 1, the system enforces **Productive Friction**:

1. **Audit missing context against a required schema.**
2. **Interview and prod the human first** (3 to 5 sharp, multiple-choice or targeted Socratic questions, challenging vague assumptions).
3. **Confirm constraints and edge cases explicitly.**
4. **Only then execute against codified rules and templates.**

In late 2026, this pattern became first-class across both open-standard **`SKILL.md` directories** (portable instruction + script + reference folders) and protocol-level **MCP `Elicitation`** (where an MCP server pauses tool execution to request structured clarification from the human operator).

## Copy-Paste Template: The Socratic Intake & Process Extraction Skill

Save this reusable `SKILL.md` inside any Claude Cowork, Claude Code, OpenCode, or workspace project folder to turn undocumented tribal knowledge into repeatable SOPs:

```markdown
---
name: socratic-process-architect
description: Interviews a subject-matter expert before drafting any deliverable or SOP, surfacing edge cases, failure modes, and hard business boundaries.
---

# Socratic Process & Deliverable Architect

## Hard Gate (Never Skip)
Do NOT draft the final document, policy, contract memo, or workflow on your first turn.

## Step 1: Diagnostic Interview (One Focused Round)
Inspect the user's request against existing workspace files, then ask 3 to 5 specific questions covering:
1. **Success & Failure Definition:** What specific error would make this output unusable or embarrassing in front of a client, CFO, or regulator?
2. **Hard Thresholds:** What dollar caps, timeline SLAs, or non-negotiable terms apply?
3. **The 10% Edge Case:** Walk me through the last time this process went sideways in real life. What signal warned you?
4. **Approval Boundary:** Which actions stay strictly draft-only for human sign-off?

## Step 2: Play Back & Codify
Summarize the extracted rules in 6 bullet points. Once confirmed, generate:
1. The requested deliverable grounded strictly in stated facts (marking any unknown `[NEEDS VERIFICATION]`).
2. A reusable `rules/<workflow-name>.md` checklist so nobody has to re-explain these rules next week.
```

## Using Interview Harnesses to Document Your Company in One Week

Have each department lead (Sales, Finance, Legal, Delivery) spend **25 minutes** being interviewed by this skill while walking through one recurring task. By Friday, your company owns documented, machine-readable operating playbooks that previously lived only inside senior employees' heads.
