**TL;DR.** AI Dev 26 in SF (Pier 48 Shed B, April 28-29) was production-engineering-heavy and laboratory-light. Below are the 10 takeaways I would brief a lead engineer on, ranked by impact on what you build next quarter. Every item links the deeper post on this site.

## 1. Marc Brooker (AWS): the limit on agentic AI is the defect rate, not capability.

The clearest framing of the conference. Capability exploded over the last 18 months. So did the defect rate. The opportunity now compounds faster from improvements in defects than from frontier moves. Brooker's working model: agents are interesting because they are a feedback loop, so we need benchmarks that capture failure severity, not just density. AWS open-sourced Trusted Remote Execution the same week, with Cedar as the policy language and autoformalization to translate natural-language SOPs into mathematically precise specs. Take the worst days as seriously as the best. [Defects, not capability, is the wall](/blog/death-of-the-junior-dev).

## 2. Andrew Ng: PM is the new bottleneck.

When the SWE feedback loop speeds up 10x, every adjacent function becomes the limiting reagent. The PM-engineer ratio is trending toward 1:1 in fast teams; the fastest teams collapse the two roles into the same person. Slowest teams add PM headcount to "manage" the agent and lose. Ng also previewed Context Hub (`andrewyng/context-hub`), a way to keep coding agents from hallucinating deprecated APIs by serving the latest, canonical building-block docs. Free for the open ones, npm-installable. [The death of the junior dev is overstated](/blog/death-of-the-junior-dev).

## 3. Paige Bailey (Google DeepMind): 75% of code at Google is AI-authored.

Last week, not aspirational. The number is meaningless without the rest of the talk. Gemini 3 ships natively multimodal (audio output, screen share, video input). Gemma 4 ships in four sizes including a 26B MoE and a 31B multimodal Dense. Genie 3 generates playable worlds from a prompt with no physics engine, dynamically. Robotics in the Mountain View campus runs Gemini directly. The frontier is shifting from "what can the model do" to "what can the model do on a Raspberry Pi running a Pucker robot."

## 4. Anush Elangovan (AMD): K-shaped future of software, ROCm as the unified layer.

System thinking, judgment, and stakeholder alignment are rising. Syntax memorization, boilerplate production, and isolated coding speed are falling. AMD's contribution to the upper arm: HotSwap (a "Rosetta for GPUs" that retargets ISA at runtime so old hardware runs new kernels), a native HIP backend for llama.cpp with zero ROCm-library dependencies, and the IREE Tokenizer aimed at the actual bottleneck for production agentic chat. The agent-as-cron-job era is over; agents are now contiguous systems that monitor, file PRs, validate tests, and merge with little review. The pipeline that supports them needs to be just as continuous. [Why I bet on a Framework laptop in 2026](/blog/framework-for-builders) is the consumer-side version of this argument.

## 5. Jeff Huber (Chroma): context rot is the failure mode nobody is naming.

Language models are not invariant to their context length. Practitioners tuned to evals do not trust past 40K tokens. This single observation kills "stuff a million-token window with the whole repo" as a strategy. The cure is context engineering plus agentic search: a search agent (Chroma's Context-1, 20B parameters running on Cerebras at 3,000 tok/s) that decides what to retrieve, plus sub-agents that encapsulate context for specialist tasks. Pareto-frontier numbers Huber compared: Opus-class at 40 tok/s and 250ms search at $25/M output tokens, vs. Context-1 at 3,000 tok/s and 30ms search at $1/M. [Long-term memory for agents](/blog/long-term-memory-for-agents) walks the architectural patterns.

## 6. Bain (Sanjin Bicanic + Xun Yang): the 8-subgraph LangGraph payroll system at 98% accuracy on 3,000 live emails per day.

Single most useful production reference of the conference. Bain decomposed an HR-services payroll workflow (handles 1-in-10 private US payrolls) into eight LangGraph subgraphs, each with scoped tools and its own eval harness. Shadow testing was the highest-leverage decision they made: emails duplicated once for human, once for AI, AI on read-only, every action recorded for offline auto-eval. Accuracy went 70% → 98% over four weeks of shadow only after offline evals had said "ready." Edge cases (not demos) decide whether agentic systems survive production. The agent is 20% of the work; the 80% is the durable scaffolding around it. [Swarm vs monolith](/blog/swarm-vs-monolith) walks the architecture.

## 7. Matthew Xu (Agentic Fabriq): OAuth was built for three actors. Agents make four.

The four-legged identity problem is now the default, not the edge case. User → Agent → MCP → API breaks the OAuth assumption that the entity making the call is the one holding the access token. Token exchange (RFC 8693) is the cleanest fix; combined with RFCs 9728 (resource metadata), 8414 (auth-server discovery), and 7591 (dynamic client registration), it completes the OAuth story for agents. Build a broker layer that handles exchange, scope narrowing, and audit. Skipping this ships agents with shared God-mode credentials and no chain of custody. [The 4-legged identity, long version](/blog/the-4-legged-identity).

## 8. Tushar Jain (Docker): per-agent MicroVMs are the security baseline.

Docker showed `docker/cagent` as the OSS reference: hard security boundary per agent, FS and network control by default, scoped credentials. Curl to pastebin.com is blocked unless you add it to policy. The blast radius of a compromised agent becomes the sandbox, not the host. Jain's framing: sandboxes are blast walls, not steering wheels. Pair with scoped credentials. As subagent orchestration scales, every subagent needs its own sandbox; your laptop will not hold hundreds, the cloud will. [Ethical autonomy](/blog/ethical-autonomy) covers the patterns.

## 9. Adit Abraham (Reducto) + Jerry Liu (LlamaIndex): doc OCR in 2026 is hybrid CV plus VLM, with agentic verification between every step.

Roughly 90% of enterprise data lives in PDFs, scans, and screenshots that were not designed for machines to read. Pure-VLM pipelines hallucinate; pure-CV pipelines miss the long tail. The 2026 winning shape is deterministic computer vision for layout and tables, vision-language models for handwriting and charts, agent-in-the-loop verification, with classification and document splitting before parsing. Liu released LiteParse (`run-llama/liteparse`, MIT-licensed, CLI-native), and the team launched ParseBench (`parsebench.ai`) as the new enterprise OCR benchmark with 2K human-verified pages. [Document OCR for agentic workflows](/blog/document-ocr-for-agents).

## 10. Andi Partovi (Veris AI): every agent that takes real-world action lives in a POMDP and needs a simulation sandbox.

Agents that move money, send emails, or edit databases cannot be tested with golden datasets. They live in a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process, not an MDP, which means the right answer is post-hoc, computed against state changes, not pre-labeled. Real users are unpredictable; tools are flaky; labels are dynamic. The teams shipping autonomous agents into regulated enterprises built simulation environments that look like production but are not real. The teams that did not are explaining to their customers why their bot invented a usage policy. [Why every agent needs a simulation sandbox](/blog/why-every-agent-needs-a-simulation-sandbox).

## What did not get enough airtime

Two things missing from the program: (a) honest dollar amounts on inference at production agentic scale, and (b) what happens to agentic loops when the model provider has an outage. Both are the questions you should be answering internally because nobody answered them publicly. [Cost discipline](/blog/token-budgeting-for-startups) and [the hybrid local-cloud failover pattern](/blog/local-vs-cloud-inference) are the two posts I would read.

## The one thing to take to the team

Build the eval harness this quarter. Not the orchestrator, not the agent, not the prompt library. The harness. Every other architectural decision compounds on top of measurement, and the teams that have measurement are visibly pulling ahead.
